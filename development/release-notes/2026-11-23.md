# Release Notes: 23 November 2026

## What a Region's Economy Is Made Of

`measurement` can now read the structure of a region's economy ([decision 0018](../decisions/0018-the-structure-of-regional-economies.md)): how its employment changed against its nation's, split into what the nation's growth, the region's mix of industries and its own competitiveness account for; how varied it is, and how much of that variety lies among related industries and how much among unrelated ones; which industries lie near those it already holds; and how complex its set of activities is, by the economic complexity index and by fitness. Each identity is exact, and an economy whose industries fall apart into separate groups is refused a complexity index, since the index would only name the groups.

## How Populations Revise What They Do

`games` can now follow a population as its members revise their strategies ([decision 0017](../decisions/0017-population-games-and-evolutionary-dynamics.md)): by imitating those who do better (the replicator), by choosing the best (best response) or a noisy best (logit), by comparing with the average (Brown, von Neumann and Nash) or with each alternative (Smith). Each is given by the rule an agent follows when it revises and by the motion of the population's shares that the rule implies, and the two are checked to agree. Whether a dynamic moves toward better payoffs and rests only at equilibria is answered at any state; in games whose externalities defeat themselves each dynamic has a quantity that never rises along its paths; and a population of finitely many agents is simulated exactly, its path staying within half a percent of the motion of shares at twenty thousand agents.

The classic cases are reproduced: the replicator circling forever in Rock–Paper–Scissors, spiralling in or out as winning pays more or less than losing costs, Hawks settling at the share V/C, and a strategy that is always worse than its twin surviving under the Smith dynamic in a fifth of the population.
