# Release Notes: 19 November 2026

## Voting Power, Bargaining and the Nucleolus

`games` can now say how much a decision rule gives each member, how two parties might settle, and how a coalition might share what it makes ([decision 0014](../decisions/0014-voting-power-bargaining-and-the-nucleolus.md)).

- **Voting power.** A rule is a simple game: the coalitions that can act. For any rule, the a priori power of each member by Penrose's and Banzhaf's measures, Shapley and Shubik's, and Coleman's power to prevent and to initiate action, with the rule's own power to act. Weighted rules with several majorities are counted by generating functions, so that bodies of hundreds are exact. Penrose's square-root rule gives weights for delegations by population. The Security Council's swings are held to their closed form, and Nassau County's board shows members with weight but no power.
- **Bargaining.** Nash's solution, Kalai and Smorodinsky's and the egalitarian one, on a frontier given by points or by a function, each checked against the axioms that characterise it and shown failing the ones it does not. Adjusted Winner divides issues between two parties with equal points and at most one issue split, and is checked efficient against a linear programme.
- **The core and the nucleolus.** Whether a game's core is empty, the least core, and the nucleolus by a sequence of linear programmes. On 150 problems of dividing an estate short of its claims, the nucleolus equals the Talmud's rule.
