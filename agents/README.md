# agents: Agent-Based Models

*Status: draft (2026-11-05): schedules, draws by identity, the event log, Gillespie's simulation, ensembles, spaces, logit choice, Schelling's model ([decision 0004](../development/decisions/0004-agent-based-models.md)) and Kirman's herding ([decision 0005](../development/decisions/0005-kirmans-herding.md)).* Agents that plan, calibration and social force are planned.

## 1. Concepts

An agent-based model is a Markov process on configurations, generated by local rules: each rule reads an agent's neighbourhood and changes that agent and what it touches. The **schedule** says how the rules compose, and it is part of the model.

- Synchronous updating is a product of kernels.
- Random sequential updating is a composition.
- Asynchronous updating in continuous time is a sum of generators: a jump process, whose law is the master equation. Small models are checked against it exactly, large ones against their mean field (Kurtz 1970).

Every draw is keyed by what it is for, (seed, agent, tick), on calx's counter-based streams, so a synchronous run does not depend on the number of threads. A run writes an **event log**, which a domain reads into its record of events. A simulated history is then analysed as an observed one.

Nothing names a society: agents, cells and options are numbered, and their meaning is the domain's.

## 3. Checks

`check_nomos_agents` (16):
- the rate tree against a linear search;
- Gillespie's SIS of 5 agents against the master equation's exact law (physis `evolve`);
- Kurtz's mean field on 1000 agents;
- the voter model's martingale;
- synchronous ticks the same on one thread and four;
- Schelling's sorting at thresholds 0, ⅓ and ½, and its ODD description;
- Kirman's herding: the herd's shape, min(k, N − k), over 200,000 units of time against the exact birth–death law, bimodal below ε = δ/(N − 1);
- logit choice and its draws;
- the plane's radius queries against brute force.

## 4. Dependencies

calx `random` (streams, categorical draws), `scheduler` (the pool). The checks also use physis `statistical_mechanics` and calx `linalg`.

## 6. Open Questions

1. **Agents that plan.** An agent may solve a POMDP about what it cannot see, through tyche's `decision`. The interface between a planning agent and the schedule (a plan per tick, or online) is open.
2. **Calibration.** Indirect inference and ABC are tyche's to write, with this engine as their simulator.
3. **Identity under births and deaths.** Agents are indices. A population that changes needs identities that survive reordering, so that their streams stay their own.
