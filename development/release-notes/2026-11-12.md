# 2026-11-12

## Claims on a Short Estate, and the Shapley Value

nomos can now divide what is too little among those with claims on it, and value each player of a coalitional game ([decision 0011](../decisions/0011-claims-and-the-shapley-value.md)).

- **The Shapley value.** For a game that gives a worth to every set of up to 24 players, each player's value is the mean of what it adds over all orders of arrival, computed exactly.
- **Dividing a short estate.** An estate and claims that exceed it can be divided by priority in an order (with the least and the greatest each claimant can get by order alone), by the mean over all orders, in proportion, by equal awards or equal losses, and by the Talmud's rule, which gives (50, 75, 75) of 200 against claims of 100, 200 and 300.
- **Paid together.** Claimants paid by turns in small steps get what the rates of the turns decide: in proportion to their claims at rates in proportion, equal awards at equal rates. "Paid together" is a family of rules, not one.

It is held to 28 checks: the glove game, the value's axioms on random games, Aumann and Maschler's table, consistency with the contested garment on every pair, and brute force over every order.

## Spatial Interaction and Congestion

nomos can now say how flows between places fall with the cost between them, and how travellers who share a congested network choose their ways ([decision 0010](../decisions/0010-spatial-interaction-and-congestion.md)).

- **Flows between places.** A new module, `interaction`, holds Wilson's models of commuting, shopping, migration or trade: with one margin fixed or both, balanced by Furness's method. Calibrated to an observed table, the decay is the Poisson maximum likelihood estimate, and agrees with statsmodels. With them come accessibility as a logsum, the common part of commuters as a measure of fit, and Lowry's model of a metropolis with Garin's closed form.
- **Congestion.** `games` now holds routing games. Given roads whose times rise as they fill and the journeys asked of them, it finds the flows at which no traveller can save time by changing route (Wardrop's equilibrium), the flows least in total time, and the price of the difference. It reproduces Pigou's and Braess's examples and scipy's solution of a grid of streets.
- **Both at once.** Evans's combined equilibrium finds where travellers go and the way they take together, with a bound that says how far from the answer each step is.
